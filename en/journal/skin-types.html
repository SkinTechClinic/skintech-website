Skin type I to VI: why it matters for your treatment

Not every skin responds the same way. We explain why your skin type decides how a laser is set.

Do you burn quickly in the sun, or do you tan easily? The answer says something about your skin type. Dermatologists use the Fitzpatrick scale for this, and in laser hair removal it is one of the first things we look at. This is how the scale works, and what it does and does not tell you.

Where the scale comes from

The American dermatologist Thomas Fitzpatrick devised the classification in 1975. He was looking for a way to choose the right dose of UV light in light therapy, because people who looked alike did not always tolerate the same dose. At first there were four types, meant for light skin. The scale was later extended to six.

The six skin types

  • Type I. Always burns and does not tan. Often very fair skin, freckles and red or light blond hair.
  • Type II. Burns quickly and tans slowly. Fair skin.
  • Type III. Sometimes burns, then tans. Lightly tinted skin.
  • Type IV. Hardly ever burns and tans easily. Light brown skin.
  • Type V. Rarely burns and tans dark brown. Brown skin.
  • Type VI. Almost never burns. Dark brown to black skin.

The classification is about how your skin responds to sunlight, and that is linked to the amount of pigment (melanin) in your skin. Your skin type is fixed from birth, according to the dermatology reference DermNet.

A tool, not a label

Your type is usually worked out with a few questions about burning and tanning. That makes the scale subjective. The Dutch Society for Dermatology and Venereology (NVDV) calls skin type only a rough indication of how quickly someone burns, with a lot of overlap between the types.

The scale does not measure skin colour or origin either. An overview by the American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) points out that people are often put in the wrong type, mostly among the darker types, and that the scale was originally made for light skin. So two people with the same skin colour can have a different type.

Why it matters in laser hair removal

A hair removal laser acts on pigment. The light has to end up in the pigment of the hair, but dark skin also has a lot of pigment in its top layer. That is why skin type helps in choosing the wavelength, energy, pulse duration and cooling. Dark skin also develops pigment marks more easily after an injury, DermNet writes. For the darkest skin types, treating a test spot first is advised.

The GentleMax Pro Plus, the device we are going to work with, has two lasers: an alexandrite laser (755 nm) and an Nd:YAG laser (1064 nm). In its American clearance the device is intended for all skin types, from I to VI, including tanned skin. Lasers with a long wavelength, such as the Nd:YAG, are considered the best choice for dark skin. You can read more in our article on laser hair removal for dark skin.

How we will look at your skin

SkinTech opens in Amsterdam in the second quarter of 2027. A laser course with us will include a consultation and a patch test: a trial treatment on a small patch of skin. We ask how your skin responds to the sun, look at your skin and your hair, and set the laser again at every session.

A tan counts too. Do not come to a treatment with tanned skin, and protect the treated skin well against the sun afterwards. The NVDV advises doing that for up to twelve weeks after the treatment.

And the sun itself?

Your skin type also says something about sun protection: fair skin needs the most. The NVDV names shade and clothing as the first step. For leisure and holidays the society advises a sunscreen with factor 30, applied thickly and again every few hours.
