The Sylton Observ 520x: your skin in focus

What a skin scan shows, what it does not, and why every course with us starts with one.

Every course at SkinTech will start with a free skin scan. For that we use the Observ 520x by Sylton, a camera system that photographs your face in different kinds of light. This is what such a scan shows, and what it does not.

What is the Observ 520x?

The Observ 520x is a skin analysis system from the Dutch company Sylton. You rest your chin on a support and the device takes a series of photos of your face, each in a different kind of light. According to Sylton that takes ten seconds on average. The images appear on an iPad.

The chin rest puts your head in the same position every time: straight on, at an angle and in profile. That makes photos from different moments easy to compare.

The light modes

Sylton describes ten light modes. Which of them are available depends on the package that comes with the device. According to the manufacturer this is what they show:

  • Daylight. The skin as you see it in normal light. This is the starting point.
  • Surface texture. Fine lines and the smoothness of the skin.
  • Pigmentation. Differences in skin tone, uneven pigment and traces of the sun.
  • Redness. Areas where the skin is red.
  • Parallel polarised light. Strengthens the reflection on the skin surface, so texture and glow are easier to see.
  • Cross polarised light. Takes away the shine, so you look a little deeper into the skin.
  • True UV. Pigment that lies under the surface.
  • Simulated Wood's light. A picture of where the skin is oilier or drier.
  • Two 360 modes. Light from an adjustable direction, in colour or in greyscale, brings out relief and contours.

What a scan is and is not

A scan shows. It does not diagnose. We go through the images together, and they say something about care and about which treatment suits your skin. Are you worried about a spot or a mole? See your GP about it. A skin scan is not meant for that.

There is still little research on the device. In a 2024 study in the journal Scientific Reports, among sixty adults at a hospital in Taiwan, its measurements of pigment and redness correlated with those of two established instruments. For pigment they also correlated with the assessment of dermatologists. That correlation was moderate, measurements were taken once, and one of the four authors is affiliated with Sylton.

What it gives you

The scan is a starting point. Together with the conversation it helps us draw up a care or treatment plan. It is also a baseline: if you come back later, we put the new images next to the old ones.

The skin scan with consultation is free and without obligation and takes about 45 minutes. You go home with a written care plan.
