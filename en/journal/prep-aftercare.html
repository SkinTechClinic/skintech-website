Preparation and aftercare: how to get the most from your treatment

The right preparation and aftercare make all the difference. Our checklist for before and after every session.

What you do before and after a laser treatment makes a difference to your skin. This is the checklist for laser hair removal. For each part you can read which source the advice comes from.

In the weeks before

  • No waxing or epilating. Stop at least four weeks before the treatment. The laser needs the hair in the follicle. Shaving is fine.
  • Out of the sun. Avoid the sun and sunbeds, do not use self-tanner and use a sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher. Pigment spots can form on tanned skin after the treatment.
  • Medicines and pregnancy. At the intake, say which medicines you use and whether you are pregnant.

This advice comes from the Mayo Clinic and the Jeroen Bosch Hospital in the Netherlands.

The day before

Shave the area to be treated, carefully, one day in advance. Both the Mayo Clinic and the Jeroen Bosch Hospital advise this.

During the treatment

You wear protective glasses and the skin is cooled. Each pulse feels like a short snap. Say so if it becomes too much.

The first days after

  • Cool. The skin can be red, swollen or irritated for a few hours. Cool it with cold water or a cold pack.
  • Pat, do not rub. Pat the skin dry and do not use perfumed products for the first few days.
  • Shaving only. The treated hairs fall out in the days to weeks that follow. Shaving is fine, waxing and epilating are not, also not between sessions.

The sun, weeks later too

Protect the treated skin from the sun. The sources differ on how long. The Jeroen Bosch Hospital mentions three weeks with an unperfumed sunscreen of at least SPF 40, the Mayo Clinic six weeks with SPF 30 or higher. The Dutch Society for Dermatology and Venereology (NVDV) advises protecting the treated skin well for up to twelve weeks to prevent dark spots. Follow what the person who treats you advises.

When to get in touch

Mild irritation and swelling usually fade within a few hours. If you get blisters, crusts or a discolouration that does not fade, contact the person who treated you.
