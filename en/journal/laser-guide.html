Laser hair removal: everything you need to know before your first session

From preparation to aftercare — a complete overview of what to expect from laser hair removal with the GentleMax Pro Plus.

Thinking about laser hair removal? Then you will want to know how it works, how many sessions you need, how to prepare and what to expect afterwards. It is all set out below. For each part you can read which source the information comes from.

How laser hair removal works

A laser sends a concentrated beam of light into the skin. The pigment in the hair, melanin, absorbs that light. The light turns into heat, and the heat damages the hair follicle. That slows hair growth, as the Mayo Clinic explains. The principle is called selective photothermolysis and was described in 1983 by Anderson and Parrish in the journal Science.

Because the laser acts on pigment, dark hairs respond best. Blond, white, grey and red hairs respond poorly, according to the Mayo Clinic and the Dutch Society for Dermatology and Venereology (NVDV).

Which lasers there are

Three lasers are mainly used for hair removal: the alexandrite laser (755 nm), the diode laser (around 800 nm) and the Nd:YAG laser (1064 nm). Which one fits depends on your skin and your hair. Dark skin needs the right laser and the right settings, the Mayo Clinic writes. According to the NVDV some devices are more suitable for it than others.

The GentleMax Pro Plus, the device we are going to work with, has an alexandrite laser and an Nd:YAG laser in one machine. A cooling spray cools the top layer of the skin with every pulse.

How many sessions, and why

Hairs grow in phases and are never all in the growth phase at the same time. The laser only works well on hairs that are in it at that moment. That is why you always need a series of treatments. The Mayo Clinic mentions four to eight treatments several weeks apart, the Jeroen Bosch Hospital in the Netherlands five to seven. We plan on six to eight sessions, four to eight weeks apart, depending on the body area.

What you can expect

Laser hair removal reduces hair growth for a long time. Nobody can promise that every hair stays away for good. A review of thirty studies found evidence, after repeated treatments, of a partial hair reduction that lasts longer than six months, and no evidence of complete and lasting hair removal (Haedersdal and Wulf, 2006).

How much hair goes differs from study to study and from person to person. In a review of the records of 89 people, mean hair reduction was 74 per cent after an average of 5.6 treatments with an alexandrite laser (Eremia and colleagues, 2001). A Cochrane review of randomised trials arrived at about half in 2006, measured up to six months after treatment. Figures like these do not predict your result.

What comes back is often finer and lighter, and sometimes an occasional maintenance treatment is needed. Laser does not treat the cause of excess hair growth, so hair growth driven by hormones can continue (Mayo Clinic).

How to prepare

  • Shave the area the day before the treatment.
  • Do not wax or epilate in the four weeks before. Shaving is fine.
  • Avoid the sun and sunbeds and do not use self-tanner. Pigment spots can form on tanned skin.
  • At the intake, say which medicines you use and whether you are pregnant. The Jeroen Bosch Hospital, for example, does not treat during pregnancy.
  • Tattooed skin is not treated with the laser.

This advice comes from the Mayo Clinic and the Jeroen Bosch Hospital.

During the treatment

You wear protective glasses and the skin is cooled. Each pulse feels like a short snap. The Mayo Clinic compares it to a rubber band flicking against your skin, the Jeroen Bosch Hospital calls it unpleasant. A small area such as the upper lip takes a few minutes, a large area such as the back can take an hour or more.

After the treatment

  • The skin can be red, swollen or irritated for a few hours. Cooling helps.
  • Protect the treated skin from the sun. The Mayo Clinic mentions six weeks and a daily sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher, the Jeroen Bosch Hospital three weeks with SPF 40 or higher. Follow what the person who treats you advises.
  • The treated hairs fall out in the days to weeks that follow. Shaving is fine, waxing and epilating are not.
  • Do not use perfumed products on the treated skin for the first few days.

Side effects and safety

Common and temporary: mild irritation, swelling and a change in skin colour. Less common: blisters, crusts, scars or a change in skin texture. Rare: thicker hair growth around the treated area. The risk is higher with dark skin and if you do not avoid the sun (Mayo Clinic). The area around the eyes is not treated.

The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) warns that in inexperienced hands laser hair removal can lead to burns, lasting changes in skin colour and scars. So always ask who will treat you and with which device.
